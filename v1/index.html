Fake CEO, Real Loss: Man Arrested After ৳2 Lakh Scam

A stolen profile photo, an invented medical emergency and three trusting managers. How a fraudster borrowed a chief executive's face and was undone by his own national ID.

A case study built on a real, global fraud pattern called CEO fraud. The people, amounts and timeline are illustrative. The statistics and the law cited below are real and sourced at the end.

The short version

What happened

A stranger copied a CEO's public profile and, posing as the boss, asked three managers to send money.

The motive

Quick cash. Fooling a person is cheaper and easier than breaking into a bank's systems.

How he was caught

The money went to a mobile wallet registered to his real national ID, so police could trace and freeze it.

How to stop it

Call the executive on a known number before paying. Two people approve every transfer.

Fahim began with nothing more than a browser. He downloaded public profile photographs and personal details belonging to Farhan Ahmed, chief executive of a Dhaka company, from LinkedIn. Nothing was hacked. Everything he needed was already on display.

He then opened a Facebook account in the CEO's full name, dressed in the company's corporate branding. To a casual glance, the profile was convincing.

“Nothing was hacked. It was purely social engineering using public data.”The Cyber Times Investigations Desk

Three department managers received urgent direct messages. The sender claimed an unexpected medical emergency while travelling abroad on business. Fear for the boss, and the habit of obeying him, did the rest. The managers sent BDT 2 Lakhs through mobile banking.

The fraud surfaced only when the real CEO returned to the office. The company reported the profile, and cyber police traced the receiving wallet to Fahim's real national ID registration. He was arrested and charged.

The psychologyWhy it works: four pressure levers

The scammer needs no technical skill. He only needs the victim to stop thinking and start obeying. Each lever below removes one chance to double-check.

Authority“It's the boss.”People rarely question a CEO. Counter: a real boss will not mind a verification call.
Urgency“Do it now.”Haste leaves no time to think. Counter: any rush to move money is itself a warning.
Fear“Medical emergency.”Worry for someone makes people skip checks. Counter: a real emergency can survive two minutes.
Secrecy“Tell no one.”Secrecy stops colleagues from spotting the lie. Counter: always ask a second person.

It is a global crime, not a one-off

This tactic is known as business email compromise, or CEO fraud. In the United States alone, the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center recorded:

How he did it · Step 1Collecting what was already public

The profile photos, full name and company details were visible to anyone. The scammer only had to copy them. Limiting what executives post about themselves, and where, shrinks the raw material for the next fake profile.

Following the Money

Mobile banking is instant and hard to reverse. That speed helped the scammer, but the registration rules behind it are what ended the scheme.

  1. Step 1Three managers send money via mobile banking
  2. Step 2Funds land in a single wallet
  3. Step 3The wallet is linked to a registered NID
  4. ResultIdentity matched. Wallet frozen.

NID-registered wallets (bKash, Nagad, Rocket) are direct electronic evidence, which is why the wallet was frozen and the sender identified.

The Charges

Tap a charge for the plain-language version.

Penal Code 1860 · §416 & §419Cheating by personation

In plain words: pretending to be someone else to deceive people.

Here: posing as CEO Farhan Ahmed to contact employees.

Penalty (§419): up to 3 years, a fine, or both.

Penal Code 1860 · §420Cheating and obtaining property

In plain words: tricking people into handing over money.

Here: three managers sent ৳2 lakh because of the lie.

Penalty: up to 7 years and a fine.

Penal Code 1860 · §468 & §471Forgery for cheating (possible)

In plain words: faking material to cheat, then using it as genuine.

Here: a counterfeit profile with the company's branding. Whether this applies depends on how a court treats digital material.

Penalty (§468): up to 7 years and a fine.

Cyber Security Act 2026Cyber fraud

In plain words: using digital means to deceive for gain, including assuming another person's identity.

Here: the fake profile and the emergency story.

Note: the older 2023 identity-fraud sections were repealed in May 2025, and the 2026 Act is being amended again. Check the current text for penalties.

Money Laundering Prevention Act 2012Receiving the proceeds

In plain words: collecting or moving money gained from a crime.

Here: the stolen money went to a wallet tied to the suspect's own ID, which made it easy to trace and freeze.

Bangladesh's cyber law has changed three times since 2023: the 2023 Act, the 2025 Ordinance, then the 2026 Act. This page is general information, not legal advice. Sentences are decided by a court.

The Punishment

  • Cheating by personation: up to 3 years, a fine, or both
  • Cheating and obtaining property: up to 7 years and a fine
  • Forgery for cheating, if proven: up to 7 years and a fine
  • Cyber and money-laundering penalties depend on the current text of the law
  • The wallet can be frozen and the money returned to the company
  • A conviction creates a criminal record
